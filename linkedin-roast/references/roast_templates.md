# LinkedIn Roast Templates & Joke Patterns

## Headline Roast Templates

**Buzzword Counter**:
> "Your headline has [X] buzzwords in [Y] words. That's a buzzword-per-word ratio of [Z]%. The Geneva Convention considers this verbal warfare."

**The Clarification Request**:
> "Your headline says '[their headline]'. I read it five times and I still don't know what you do. Are you a consultant? A wizard? A professional sentence builder?"

**The Comparison**:
> "Your headline reads like someone threw a business textbook into a blender with a motivational poster."

**The Generic Callout**:
> "You could swap your headline with any other '[industry]' professional on LinkedIn and nobody would notice. You're the human equivalent of a stock photo."

## Photo Roast Templates

**Missing Photo**:
> "No photo? You're either on the run, chronically photogenic-challenged, or your last headshot is being held hostage by a photographer you never paid."

**Arms Crossed**:
> "Ah, the classic 'arms crossed' power pose. Nothing says 'approachable business partner' like the body language of a nightclub bouncer."

**Conference Stage**:
> "Your profile photo is you on a stage somewhere. We get it, you spoke once at an event with 12 attendees. That doesn't make you Tony Robbins."

**Outdated Photo**:
> "Based on the resolution and fashion choices, I'm guessing this photo was taken sometime between 2008 and the invention of front-facing cameras."

**Too Casual**:
> "Your profile photo looks like it was cropped from a beach vacation. Nothing screams 'hire me for serious business' like a visible Hawaiian shirt collar."

## About Section Roast Templates

**Too Long**:
> "Your About section is [X] words. That's not a summary, that's a memoir. Even your autobiography would have an abridged version."

**Too Short**:
> "Your About section is [X] words. I've seen more detailed fortune cookie messages. What are you hiding?"

**Third Person**:
> "You wrote your About section in third person. '[Name] is a passionate professional...' Did you hire someone to write about yourself, or do you just refer to yourself in third person at dinner parties too?"

**Humble Brag Pattern**:
> "Your About section contains [X] humble brags. 'I reluctantly accepted my award...' We get it, you're great. Subtlety isn't your strength."

**Missing CTA**:
> "Your About section has no call to action. What do you want people to do after reading this? Just... admire you from afar?"

## Experience Roast Templates

**Vague Accomplishments**:
> "'Drove growth.' 'Led initiatives.' 'Spearheaded innovation.' These aren't accomplishments, these are word clouds. Did you actually DO anything, or just supervise nouns?"

**Title Inflation**:
> "In 5 years, you went from 'Coordinator' to 'Senior Director of Strategic Innovation.' That's not a career, that's a title Pokémon evolution."

**Job Hopping**:
> "You've had [X] jobs in [Y] years. Your LinkedIn is less 'career journey' and more 'employment speed dating.'"

**Founder of Everything**:
> "You're listed as 'Founder' of [X] companies. Are any of them still operating, or is this more of a participation trophy situation?"

**Missing Metrics**:
> "'Increased efficiency.' By how much? 1%? 1000%? The mystery is killing me. Without numbers, this is just creative writing."

## Skills & Endorsements Roast Templates

**Microsoft Office**:
> "You have 47 endorsements for Microsoft Excel. We've all opened a spreadsheet, my friend. That's not a skill, that's clicking an icon."

**Irrelevant Skills**:
> "Your skills include '[weird skill]'. I'm dying to know when that last came up in a quarterly review."

**Endorsement Farming**:
> "You have 99+ endorsements for '[skill]' from people who have never seen you do it. LinkedIn endorsements: where reciprocity meets delusion."

**Skill Overload**:
> "You've listed [X] skills. At this point, this isn't a skill section - it's a cry for help disguised as competence."

## Activity/Post Roast Templates

**Engagement Bait**:
> "Your posts end with 'Agree? 👇' more often than actual sentences. That's not engagement, that's peer pressure with emojis."

**LinkedIn Influencer**:
> "You posted [X] times this month. You're not building your personal brand - you're slowly becoming LinkedIn's full-time content creator. Does your actual job know about this?"

**Motivational Content**:
> "Your feed is 80% motivational quotes over stock photos of sunrises. LinkedIn isn't Pinterest. We can tell the difference."

**Humble Brag Posts**:
> "'I'm humbled to announce...' followed by a brag. You know 'humbled' doesn't mean what you think it means, right? Humility and self-congratulation are mutually exclusive."

**That Story Format**:
> "Another 'inspirational story' post where you helped someone and learned a valuable lesson. Did everyone clap at the end? Did that someone's name? Albert Einstein?"

## Missing Information Templates

**No Recommendations**:
> "Zero recommendations. Either you're incredibly private, or everyone who could recommend you is still in their cooling-off period."

**Empty Featured Section**:
> "Your Featured section is empty. What are you hiding? Your best work, or evidence that you don't have any?"

**No Posts**:
> "No activity. You're a LinkedIn ghost - you haunt the platform but leave no trace. Very mysterious. Very unemployable-looking."

**Incomplete Profile**:
> "Your profile is [X]% complete. That's the same completion rate as my New Year's resolutions."

## Closing Burn Templates

**Career Advice**:
> "My career advice for you? Maybe try a different platform. Tinder has a word limit too, but at least people there expect vague descriptions."

**The Callback**:
> "In conclusion, you've managed to create a profile that says everything and nothing simultaneously. You're not a professional - you're a paradox with a Premium subscription."

**The Fake Compliment**:
> "On a positive note, if LinkedIn ever needs an example of 'generic profile' for their help section, you're a shoo-in."

**The Question**:
> "After reviewing your profile, I have just one question: Does your employer know you spend this much time on your personal brand instead of working?"

## Afterburn (Genuine Compliments)

Always end with something kind:

- "But for real, [specific genuine strength] shows you've got actual chops."
- "Jokes aside, [something positive about their career/experience]."
- "In all seriousness, [genuine observation] is impressive."
- "Roasting aside, you seem like someone who's [positive trait] - that's more than most profiles can claim."
- "Real talk: [authentic compliment]. The rest is just LinkedIn being LinkedIn."
